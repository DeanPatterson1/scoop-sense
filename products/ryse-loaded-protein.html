Product details

Where this label sits

RYSE Loaded Protein is one of the protein powders in the Scoop Sense database. The label uses at least one proprietary blend, so a blend total stands in place of the individual amounts inside it. It runs 27 servings per container at the full labeled serving. Everything on this page comes from the manufacturer's supplement facts panel as of September 2026 — never from the marketing page.

Key ingredients & studied doses
  • Whey protein blend (instantized whey isolate, then whey concentrate) · 25 g. Isolate is listed ahead of concentrate, and 25 g per scoop sits inside the 20–40 g per-serving range used in muscle protein synthesis research.
  • MCT powder (medium-chain triglycerides from palm kernel and coconut oil) · amount not stated on label. Listed third, ahead of the flavor system, but the panel discloses only the 2.5 g total fat figure — the MCT share of it is not broken out.
  • Organic agave inulin (prebiotic fiber) · 1 g dietary fiber per serving. Inulin is a prebiotic fiber; the 1 g on this panel is well under the several grams a day used in gut-microbiome research.
Flavors & sweetener

Eighteen-plus flavors including licensed Little Debbie, JIF and Jet-Puffed collaborations; figures verified on the Vanilla Ice Cream panel (35 g scoop, 27 servings) and cross-checked against Jet-Puffed Marshmallow (33.9 g scoop), with sucralose and acesulfame potassium doing the sweetening.

Who it's best for

A standard-ratio powder — fine as a food supplement when total daily protein is what matters. Skip it if you want every individual dose disclosed on the label. Derived from the label figures above — not medical advice.

Common questions about RYSE Loaded Protein

How much of a RYSE Loaded Protein scoop is actually protein?

25 g of protein from a 35 g serving — about 71%. The rest is flavoring, fats, carbs, and whatever else the label lists. A higher percentage means less filler per scoop.

What does the proprietary blend in RYSE Loaded Protein hide?

The Whey protein blend (instantized whey isolate, then whey concentrate) lists 25 g as a combined total. A blend total tells you the sum of its ingredients, not the individual doses inside it — which is why blend entries can't be checked against studied amounts.
